Some of the things that we want to predict, such as whether or not someone leaves the organization or whether or not someone uses a certain benefit, are dichotomous. For reasons explained below, OLS is inappropriate for such dependent variables. Instead, we can use logistic regression. To see why this is necessary, consider the following plot and the equations below it, which produce the curve and the line inside it.

Left axis: log odds. Right axis: the corresponding probability. Observed log odds in each bin use the empirical logit, ln[(k + .5)/(m − k + .5)], where k of the m cases in the bin have Y = 1, so a bin with all 0s or all 1s still has a finite value.

Analyze your own data

Fit logistic and probit regressions to your own data. Your data never leave this page: everything is computed in your browser, and nothing is uploaded or stored.

1. Enter your data

Upload a CSV or tab-delimited file, or paste the data below. The first row must contain variable names. The outcome must have exactly two distinct values (for example, 0 and 1, or yes and no). Predictors can be numeric or categorical; categorical predictors are dummy coded against a reference category you choose. Cases missing any variable in the model are dropped (listwise deletion); blank cells, NA and a single period count as missing.
